## Purpose

Use this skill to make the manuscript's argument easy to inspect. The paper
should tell a clear story: problem, gap, hypothesis, method, evidence,
limitations, and implications.

## Section Architecture

1. Introduction: hook, gap, contribution list, and roadmap.
2. Background and related work: definitions, prior systems, and what remains unresolved.
3. Method or pipeline: what is being tested and why it is the right intervention.
4. Experiments: hypotheses, setup, baselines, ablations, metrics, and statistical plan.
5. Results and analysis: evidence first, then interpretation.
6. Limitations: failure modes, scope boundaries, and threats to validity.
7. Conclusion: numbered findings and what they imply, not a repeat of the abstract.

## Paragraph Patterns

- Claim, evidence, implication: use for main body arguments.
- Compare, contrast, tradeoff: use for method and baseline comparisons.
- Concession, rebuttal: use for limitations and reviewer-sensitive claims.
- Funnel: use for introduction and motivation.

## Claim Discipline

- Use observation or conjecture when evidence is suggestive.
- Use strong language only when experiments and citations directly support it.
- Downgrade claims when the evidence is partial, underpowered, or indirect.
- Keep terminology consistent across abstract, introduction, figures, and conclusion.

## Differentiation Rules

- "More recent" is not enough. The paper needs a new angle, benchmark, taxonomy, feedback loop, or empirical test.
- If a taxonomy is used, prefer multi-axis structure over a flat list.
- Empty cells in a taxonomy should become explicit research gaps or experiment opportunities.
